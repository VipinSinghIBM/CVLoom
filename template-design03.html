Words by Your Name

About the work behind the byline.

Much of the work I care about begins with listening. Long before a report is written, a story edited, or a field project documented, there is the slower work of arrival: learning how people describe their own worlds and noticing what cannot be captured by a summary alone.

I have built a practice around patient storytelling—bringing together observation, reporting, research, and human-centered communication so that institutions, communities, and collaborators can be represented with dignity and clarity. This portfolio is designed to hold that kind of work: textured, grounded, and deliberately paced.

“The strongest stories do not shout. They stay with us because they make room for another life to fully appear.”

Whether the project is social impact writing, field reporting, documentary communication, community outreach, or editorial storytelling, I am most interested in work that treats attention as a form of care.

“Narrative work asks for patience: the courage to remain with complexity until it becomes legible.”

Work since 2018

A timeline told as short stories.

2024 — Present
Lead Narrative Consultant

Working close to communities, but writing for decision-makers.

The role has required a careful balance: staying near real lives while translating experience into reports, essays, and strategic narratives that institutions can hear. Much of the work has involved listening first, then slowly shaping material that remains faithful to the people at its center.

2021 — 2024
Editorial Research Associate

Where reporting met structure.

These years were spent building editorial discipline—fact-checking, interviewing, drafting, revising, and learning how a large story becomes readable without losing its human stakes. It was an education in pace as much as in prose.

2018 — 2021
Program Communications Writer

Learning how institutions speak, and how people actually do.

By writing across outreach material, field notes, internal summaries, and public communication, I began to understand the tension between official language and lived experience—and how narrative can gently close that distance.

“Good narrative work does not simplify people. It creates enough space for their contradictions to remain intact.”
